GIES x IBKR x Bridgewater Trading Competition

Official rules charter and governance framework. An educational initiative for University of Illinois students to gain hands-on experience in the Interactive Brokers paper trading environment. The primary goal is not simply the highest paper profits, but sound investment principles, disciplined research habits, and thoughtful risk management. Every rule and design decision serves that educational mission, and any future change must serve the same end.

About the program

The competition is organized with DLAB Consulting and Gies College of Business. You trade in IBKR’s paper environment with the same starting capital as every other team, document your reasoning, and are evaluated on both results and how clearly you explain your strategy.

  • Real-market workflow without real-money risk (paper accounts only)
  • Governance checkpoints: Rule Freeze before launch, Tech Green-Light, and a mid-competition health review
  • Aligned with university academic integrity and zero tolerance for insider trading
  • Document version 2.0 (Spring 2026) — draft, pending approval

Eligibility & competition tiers

  • The competition runs two parallel tracks for participants at different experience levels and from different backgrounds.
  • Gies Invitational: Open exclusively to currently enrolled University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign students. Intended for participants with prior coursework in finance, analytics, or investing—a more competitive, performance-focused environment.
  • A parallel track serves other experience levels; organizers publish full tier definitions before registration.
  • Teams: 2–4 members per team. All members must be currently enrolled UIUC students. Faculty and staff are not eligible to compete.
  • Teams must register by the published registration deadline. Each participant may belong to only one team.

Platform, capital & instruments

  • Platform: Interactive Brokers (IBKR) paper trading environment only. All accounts are paper—no real money. Starting capital: $100,000 virtual USD per team, equal for all teams.
  • Approved asset classes: U.S. equities listed on NYSE, NASDAQ, or AMEX; exchange-traded funds (ETFs); futures if IBKR supports them; equity options (standard expiration cycles only), subject to IBKR account settings; derivatives (e.g., futures, commodities) only if IBKR supports them and organizers enable them.
  • Prohibited instruments: Cryptocurrencies and digital assets; bonds and fixed-income securities; forex and currency trading; and any instrument not listed among the approved asset classes above.

Trading window, orders & limits

  • Trading dates: Only during the official competition period (dates to be confirmed). Trading hours: 8:30 AM–3:00 PM Central Time, Monday through Friday. No trading outside these hours or on market holidays. All timestamps are recorded in Central Time; all values are in U.S. dollars.
  • Order types permitted: Market, limit, stop-loss, and bracket orders. Both long and short positions are allowed. Margin: Not permitted unless explicitly supported and confirmed by IBKR demo account settings.
  • Daily transaction limits: Minimum transactions per day: 0 (no forced daily trading). Maximum transactions per single security per day: 25 trades. Organizers may set a weekly minimum activity threshold to prevent complete inactivity.
  • Position limits: No more than 25% of starting capital ($25,000) may be invested in any single security at any one time. Total options exposure may not exceed 50% of the portfolio at any one time.

Algorithmic trading policy

The competition rewards human research and judgment—not automated systems.

  • Strictly prohibited: Bots, scripts, macros, or any automated trading programs; algorithmic microtransaction strategies designed to exploit small price movements at high frequency; any automation that executes trades without direct human decision-making per transaction.
  • Permitted: AI tools used for research, screening, and analysis (for example ChatGPT for summarizing news, AI stock screeners)—educational use that does not automate execution.
  • Enforcement: Accounts suspected of algorithmic activity are flagged by the Tech Lead for review. Confirmed violations result in immediate disqualification.

Scoring methodology

Scoring is split equally between financial performance and quality of reasoning, reflecting the educational mission.

  • Portfolio performance (50%): Final account balance and P&L, together with key risk-adjusted performance metrics. Rankings are determined by P&L and key risk-adjusted metrics.
  • Strategy & rationale (50%): Trade memos, final presentation or summary, and quality of research cited—evaluated by a panel of judges. Judges evaluate consistency of strategy and quality of reasoning.
  • Trade memos: Five trades must be accompanied by a brief memo (2–3 sentences) submitted through the official platform or form. Each memo must state: (1) the investment thesis, (2) the financial resource(s) consulted, and (3) the expected outcome.
  • At competition close, teams must submit a 1–2 page strategy summary or short presentation outlining their overall approach.

Penalties & enforcement

  • Exceeding position limits: −5% deduction from the total P&L score per violation.
  • Code of conduct violations: point deductions or disqualification at judges’ discretion.
  • Confirmed algorithmic trading: immediate disqualification.
  • Confirmed insider trading: immediate disqualification; may be referred to university or legal authorities.

How it works

  1. Register your team (2–4 members, all enrolled UIUC students) by the published deadline. Each student may only be on one team.
  2. Onboard: Receive credentials, use the sandbox (3–5 day practice window), and attend the mandatory Rules & Ethics webinar before live trading.
  3. Trade the live window during official hours (8:30 AM–3:00 PM CT, Mon–Fri; no trading outside those hours or on market holidays).
  4. Document your work: Submit required trade memos and, at close, a 1–2 page strategy summary or short presentation.
  5. After trading ends: Accounts are frozen; top portfolios are audited; judges review rationale submissions; a final audit report is produced. An awards ceremony with IBKR networking, a post-event survey, and an operations retrospective close out the program.

Code of conduct & integrity

  • Insider trading — zero tolerance: Insider trading is illegal and will not be tolerated. Any team found using material, non-public information will be immediately disqualified; the matter may be referred to university administration or other authorities as appropriate.
  • Collusion & manipulation: Teams may not coordinate trades with other teams to manipulate leaderboard standings.
  • Account security: Teams may not share login credentials with anyone outside the team. Each team is responsible for all activity on its account.
  • Academic integrity: All trades must be executed by registered team members only. Participants must acknowledge the rulebook in writing before the competition begins. Any violation of university academic integrity policies is also a violation of this framework.

Reporting & behavior

  • Reporting: Suspicious activity must be reported immediately to the Operations Lead or via the official support channel. Reports are reviewed by the Steering Committee within 48 hours.
  • Respect: Participants and organizers must treat each other with respect. Harassment, discrimination, or abusive behavior toward others may result in disqualification.
  • Organizers are expected to apply rules consistently and without favoritism.

Governance structure

Steering Committee (strategic authority)

Holds final decision-making authority on the rulebook, dispute resolution, and long-term objectives. Includes the Student President, IBKR Sponsor Representative, and Faculty Lead / University Representative.

Core operations team

  • Operations Lead: Drives the team, manages timelines, assigns tasks, ensures deadlines are met.
  • Tech Lead: Sets up and monitors the IBKR environment, manages data feeds and leaderboards, handles technical issues.
  • Marketing Lead: Recruitment, social media, info sessions, and participant communications.
  • Sponsor Lead: Primary contact between IBKR and the operations team; may also serve as Finance Lead.

Support team

  • Judges: Define grading criteria, evaluate trade rationales, certify the final leaderboard.
  • University IT: Assists with platform access and security.
  • Faculty advisors: Ensure alignment with academic integrity standards.

A RACI-style responsibility matrix in the full published rules assigns who is responsible, accountable, consulted, or informed for budgets, platform setup, rulebook finalization, disputes, disqualification rulings, and final winner certification across the Steering Committee, Ops Lead, Tech Lead, judges, and sponsors.

Disputes, amendments & checkpoints

Dispute resolution

  1. Participant raises the issue via the official Discord/Slack support channel or by emailing the Operations Lead.
  2. Operations Lead acknowledges within 24 hours and investigates.
  3. If resolved at the operations level, the decision is documented and communicated.
  4. If unresolved, the issue is escalated to the Steering Committee within 48 hours.
  5. The Steering Committee issues a final binding ruling. No further appeals are permitted within the competition.

Amendments & interpretation

  • Proposed changes must be submitted to the Operations Lead in writing.
  • Amendments require approval from the Steering Committee and must be made before the Rule Freeze date.
  • After the Rule Freeze, no changes to the rulebook are permitted for the duration of that competition cycle.
  • The Steering Committee holds final interpretive authority over all rules. In cases of ambiguity, its ruling is binding. Rulings are documented and shared with participants.

Risk controls & checkpoints

  • Rule Freeze (2 weeks before launch): All rules are locked; no amendments after this date so the field is fair for everyone.
  • Tech Green-Light (48 hours pre-launch): The Tech Lead certifies that data feeds are live, latency is acceptable, and the platform can handle competition load.
  • Mid-competition health check (end of Week 2): Operations Lead reviews leaderboard integrity, flags suspicious activity, and confirms support channels are functioning.
  • The sandbox phase (practice before live trading) lets participants learn the system in a consequence-free environment.
  • All participants must attend or watch the mandatory Rules & Ethics webinar before trading begins.
  • All teams must sign a Rule Acknowledgement before trading begins. Clear disqualification rules for collusion and insider trading protect the university’s reputation and IBKR’s brand.

FAQ

Can I use AI?

Yes for research, screening, and analysis (for example summarizing news or using screeners). Automated trading, bots, macros, or anything that executes without direct human decision-making per trade is strictly prohibited and may lead to disqualification.

How big can my team be?

Teams must have 2–4 members. Every member must be a currently enrolled UIUC student, and each person may only compete on one team.

What can we trade?

U.S. equities, ETFs, and—when supported and enabled—futures and listed equity options. Derivatives are allowed only if IBKR supports them and organizers enable them. Cryptocurrencies, bonds, and forex are prohibited. Margin is not allowed unless explicitly supported in the IBKR demo configuration.

When can we place trades?

Only during the official competition period (dates to be confirmed), Monday through Friday, 8:30 AM–3:00 PM Central Time. No trading on market holidays or outside those hours.

How does scoring work?

Scoring is split 50% / 50%: portfolio performance (final balance, P&L, and risk-adjusted metrics) and strategy & rationale (trade memos, final presentation or summary, and research quality, judged by a panel). Position-limit violations reduce the P&L score component; code-of-conduct issues may be penalized or disqualified at judges’ discretion; confirmed algorithmic trading or insider trading results in disqualification (with possible referral in the case of insider trading).

How do I report a problem or dispute?

Use the official Discord/Slack support channel or email the Operations Lead. The Operations Lead responds within 24 hours; unresolved matters can be escalated to the Steering Committee, whose decision is final for the competition.

What does the TRACE chatbot do?

TRACE helps you navigate this site and common questions. Binding interpretations, disputes, and formal rulings follow the Operations Lead and Steering Committee process described on this page.
